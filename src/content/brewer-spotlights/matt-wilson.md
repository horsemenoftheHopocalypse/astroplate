first_batch: >-
  I started brewing in 1985 while I lived in Washington state where I was born
  and raised. My first batch was started on our kitchen cooktop in a ceramic
  canning kettle. I mixed boiling tap water with a can of malt and hop extract,
  purchased “off-the-shelf” at Albertson’s. How times were different. The
  mixture was transferred into a glass carboy of cold water and given a dose of
  Red Star yeast for two weeks until being siphoned to bottles holding a scant
  teaspoon of priming sugar. I’ll always remember the sight of the CO2 vapor as
  it curled up and out from the top of the bottle as a pop sounded with the
  removal of the cap. My mentors, a handful of men from the depression era,
  brewers of the ceramic crock and cheesecloth cover, cheered at the sight.
  According to them, this was the most important signal of a fine beer.

biggest_fail: >-
  For several years, I brewed on a homemade three kettle, gravity flow, propane
  system consisting of full-size beer kegs modified with cut-off tops, and
  stainless handles welded near the tops and ball valves fashioned near the
  bottoms. After many months of use, out of the blue, two batches of beer
  spoiled from bacterial infections. I was flabbergasted because my sanitation
  practices are a top priority. After a lot of head scratching, I decided to
  disassemble the boiling kettle’s ball valve and fittings. Here I found the
  source of the problem where the ball valve connected to a compression fitting
  securing a copper pipe used to hold the transfer hose.

brewery_setup: >-
  I stopped homebrewing in 1999 when our family moved from Washington to Fort
  Worth with my job transfer, but with my recent retirement I came back to
  brewing. In preparation, I framed in and closed off a storage area in our
  garage and built a little brewery inside to suit my needs. It has a plumbed
  sink with hot and cold water, 110 and 220-volt power outlets, a 35-gallon RO
  water system, an exhaust fan housed with a make-up air return and central air
  and heat. I brew with a 220-volt, 65-liter Brewzilla 3 in 1 system. I also
  have a 35-liter Brewzilla used mostly for sparge water. I ferment in Kegland
  Kegmenters of various sizes and Corny kegs, both 5 and 2.5 gallons in size. I
  have three chest freezers (7 cubic ft.). The set-up provides the option to
  ferment under pressure and transfer under pressure along the process.
why_homebrew: >-
  I brew for the fun and enjoyment of brewing. I like the challenges inherent in
  brewing. It’s difficult to make good beer, but when a batch turns out great,
  it feels so rewarding after all the effort expended in the process. I also
  really enjoy the social aspects of being a member in the Horsemen of the
  Hopocalypse. Our gatherings are such a great opportunity to talk about brewing
  while we share other aspects of our lives together.
