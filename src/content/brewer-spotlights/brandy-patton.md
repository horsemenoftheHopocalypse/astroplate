first_batch: >-
  Stubby’s Red IPA, I think he called it the Outlaw. I did the extract version
  on my then brand new single vessel system. I really didn’t know how to manage
  temperatures so I ended up hitting 170 or so during the steeping/mash phase.
  Dodged bees and wasps for a couple of hours, of course it boiled over almost
  instantly when I started the boil. The beer was drinkable, but oh wow I
  learned what astringency meant.
favorite_beer: >-
  The current version of my saison recipe. I've gotten dinged for carbonation,
  dryness and hop flavor many times so the last batch I doubled the carb
  pressure, added a bunch of honey and cherry puree at flameout and another
  ounce of hops. It came out at 11 SRM and IBUs so I hope it does well in the
  Austin Zealots Crusade class this year. It's also entered in our Belgian Draft
  Horse. 
biggest_fail: >-

  I decided to try making a southern hemisphere IPA with them. I didn’t think
  they’d be enough so I grabbed 4 ounces of Motueka hops. That beer was
  horrible! I don’t know what cat pee tastes like, but I bet it was close to
  what I just made five gallons of.
go_to_beer: >-

favorite_style: >-
  25B, I’ve had the best luck with Saisons. They finish fast, you don’t really
  need to worry about fermentation temperatures and they can be fairly cheap to
  make. For drinking I’m pretty predictable, I’ve got a beard, I’m wearing a
  brewery t-shirt and I like beer so of course it 21A the good old American IPA.
brewery_setup: >-
  My early attempts at all-grain were brew in a bag and for mashing I used my
  sous-vide immersion cooker to maintain the mash temperature. It actually
  worked pretty well but one day my work sent me a large bonus in the form of a
  pre-paid debit card so I used it to buy a Robobrew which after a few uses
  smoked the circuit board. So now the same system with a new circuit board is a
  Brewzilla version 3.1.1. I love brewing on this thing because it’s 110V and I
  can set it on my counter next to the kitchen sink. For fermenting I’ve got a
  chest freezer with a temperature controller to lager in, which rotates to an
  additional refrigerator when I’m not making a lager. As for fermenters I have
  a couple of the FastFerment conicals and an assortment of buckets and carboys.
  Nothing really special there. I had a kegerator already so I was kegging beer
  from the beginning. I only bottle for competitions or sharing and for that
  I’ve got one of the NukaTap counter pressure fillers. It’s made that process a
  lot faster and cleaner.
why_homebrew: >-
  I lied to myself and got into it to “save money”. Now as I catalog my
  three-tap kegerator with a nitro tap, my over a dozen corny kegs, keg cleaning
  station, fermentation freezer, bottle capper, my new mill, all the chemicals,
  jars with yeast cultures, stir plate, etc… Nevermind, I think I made my point.
  Last year I decided to finally enter Bluebonnet with a saison and my wife’s
  hot cocoa stout. While we were watching, I said if we win a medal I’m going
  all in with homebrewing, if not I’m going to find another hobby. Well darn it
  the saison got a silver medal and both beers went to the BOS rounds. Her beer
  scored 42 points! So now we’re competitive homebrewers.
