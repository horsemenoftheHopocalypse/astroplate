first_batch: >-
  My first batch was sometime in the ‘90s. I did extract brewing for a few years
  but I am certain it was pretty miserable by today's standards. I had no
  community or internet to refine my knowledge and processes. I joined the
  Horsemen shortly after retiring a year ago and started asking questions about
  what I should get for equipment. I built my kegerator first using a 7CF chest
  freezer, and put in two taps. Now it's time for beer. Still treading lightly
  on what to buy, I went to Jarrett's big brew day, brought home five gallons of
  Old Ale and did the fermentation and kegging. Meanwhile, I met the Big Cypress
  crew at Stacy Myers' crawfish boil and Stacy stepped up to mentor for me. We
  brewed a Cali Common at his place and I brought home five gallons to finish. A
  month or so later, having purchased a surplus Anvil Foundry all-in-one kit
  from John Skelton, I endeavored to brew my first solo batch. A Ful Croi Irish
  Red. I was painstakingly careful...wrote out every step. It came out great. I
  entered a later batch of this same recipe in 2023 Bluebonnet (my first
  competition) and it scored a combined 41 and made mini-BOS.
favorite_beer: >-
  This past winter, I decided to try lagering. My medieval way of getting lower
  fermentation temp is an Igloo cooler water bath and frozen water bottles to
  modulate (thanks, Chris Bart for the suggestion). Six weeks or so later, I
  learned about diacetyl when my Munich Helles had a distinct buttery taste. (It
  cleared up in the keg.) At this time, I was a week into fermenting a Helles
  Export so I raised the temperature the second week to clear up the diacetyl. 
  It worked and every time I poured one through the life of that keg I would
  think to myself: “that's a damn fine pour!” To this day, my favorite.
biggest_fail: >-
  Two, actually.

  - The day I started late in the afternoon and rushed myself and forgot to
  insert the mash tun before adding grain. I brainstormed ways to save it but
  knew I'd always question the outcome so I bailed and dumped it. 

  - I brewed an Oktoberfest and was preparing to rack to the fermenter. I
  hoisted the Anvil onto something that was inadequate for the purpose (although
  I'd done this same way several times before). I turned to get a piece of hose
  and the whole thing tipped. Five gallons of Oktoberfest wort down the
  driveway. I didn't initially want to share with club members but have since
  found everyone has a similar "oh s***!" story.
go_to_beer: >-
  If it's a long, hot day at the pool or lake...a light Anheuser-Busch product
  often makes it to my cooler. My favorite all time commercial beer is Red Hook
  ESB. Can't find it here these days but I have a clone in my kegerator as we
  speak.
fermenter_now: >-
  I have three taps on my kegerator that have Red Hook ESB, Belgian pale ale,
  and a Scottish ale. Nothing fermenting...no room at the inn.
favorite_style: >-
  I like malty-front with a bitter finish so German lagers, ESBs, and Cali
  Commons.
brewery_setup: >-
  A 10-gallon Anvil Foundry with two Fermonsters, igloo water bath, and
  three-tap kegerator. Pretty pedestrian.
why_homebrew: >-
  When I was coming up on retirement, a common question is: what are you gonna
  do with all that time? I wasn't sure either. It's such a paradigm shift from
  having worked 40 years in engineering. I enjoyed my extract brewing back in
  the ‘90s but wanted to step up quality and include all-grain. My first call
  was to Brewhound when I asked Chris about classes. He directed me to the
  Horsemen and I attended my first gathering at Cowtown the next night. I
  attribute the camaraderie and knowledge Horsemen offers as the reason I've
  stuck with it.
